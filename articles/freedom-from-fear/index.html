Freedom from Fear

Tyler Lengyel · June 7, 2026 · 3 min read

Read the original on Substack ↗

As I sit here on a Sunday morning in America, drinking my cup of coffee, learning about new things on the internet with the TV playing Perfect Match on Netflix in the Background, I came across the Universal Declaration of Human Rights that was adopted by the United Nations General Assembly in 1948. One thing that I love about Wikipedia is the countless rabbit holes you can find yourself going down, the patterns you make yourself see wether its based on ego, a memory, or something someone said one time on X. This happens to me more times than I’d like to admit, for example I started looking into the Dogon People and their history on Friday evening then ended up on Google Earth looking at Macaronesia because of what Pliny the Elder said about the Fortunate Isles by 2 in the morning. It’s fun for me to learn about new things while keeping my imagination open to mythologies and legends of the past, but it also makes you see the state of the world from a historical standpoint, then you start to ask yourself why history seems to rhyme as much as it does. To be transparent, I’m no expert on historical events of the world, let alone the full history of the country that I’ve grown up in for the past 33 years, which is one reason I’ll never try to preach to anyone about what they should believe, how they should act, or what is truth in their mind. I will, however, speak of truths based on what I do know actually happened, because death in its entirety is as real as it gets. When I came across the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, I read the brief description before scrolling down and seeing a snip titled ‘Freedom from Fear’ which is the last of four total oil paintings by Norman Rockwell. It shows parents tucking in their kids at night, showing the human side of war where life itself still goes on flor the majority around the world, and if you’re lucky enough to not be in it, you’re not having to worry about your home being bombed in the middle of the night, killing your loved ones while they sleep. This was a real reality in late 1940, London, where its stated more than a million London houses were destroyed or damaged and more than 40,000 civilians were killed. Whats sad, is this has become a new reality today, especially in Iran where the citizens have been stripped of their human rights and dignity for over 47 years, but as we sit here in America, drinking our coffee, watching the newest TV shows, preparing for the World Cup, we have brothers and sisters continuing to fight for their lives while we bitch about gas prices. As I said, I’m not going to preach to anyone, nor will I tell you how you should live your life, because you are free to do as you please, as it should be. However, just like 40,000 civilians were killed in London in 1940, there were 40,000 Iranians killed in January. As we approach the 250th year of the United States existence, I believe it’s important to reflect on the past, understanding how we got here, and more importantly understanding that wether a person is an American, Iranian, Ukrainian, or a relative you hate, we all deserve the Freedom from Fear.

Have a Sunday,

Tyler Lengyel
