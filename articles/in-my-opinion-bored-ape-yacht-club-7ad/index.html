In My Opinion: Bored Ape Yacht Club Is Suspect

Part 2

Tyler Lengyel · December 30, 2022 · 4 min read

Read the original on Substack ↗

What’s up y’all,

To start I’d like to mention that Part 1 of this series was written to try to shine light on the incompetence the team seems to have surrounding the things they do. Whether that be events, new drops for the collection, or awareness towards the ethos of the space from my perspective. The reason I bring this up is due to some readers only seeming to focus on the antisemitic traits that I pointed out in one part of the article. There’s plenty of info to read on both sides of that situation, there’s even links in the article stating both sides, so please understand that it’s only one point of many I’m trying to make.

Now that we’ve cleared that up, let’s talk about the metadata for every Bored Ape, including the Honorary Bored Apes.  The metadata, which is what makes the Bored Ape a Bored Ape, is not stored on-chain, which means that in theory some day a person’s Ape could disappear and all they would be left with is a number tied to the contract on the blockchain. This obviously isn’t very likely, but still possible, nonetheless. This is even more reason to ask why they haven’t moved them on-chain since selling out, from what I read about Moonbirds it sounds like they have the ability to move them on-chain, so why haven’t they?  On the other hand, we have the Honorary Bored Apes. These Apes were created to hand out to influencers and other individuals who helped contribute to the growth of Bored Ape Yacht Club. To see that some people have paid over $100,000 for some of these on secondary, it makes me feel scared for whoever’s holding this thinking that it’s worth something. The fact of the matter is it doesn’t take long to deploy a contract with the tools that are available, they just took the easy route. Why? My opinion is it's probably because they didn’t expect to be around too long if/when they minted out. Probably why they weren’t doxing themselves from the get-go as well.  There’s a hot take for ya.

It would be laughable to think that this project grew the way it did organically, far from it. If I’ve learned one thing in the past year regarding this space, it’d be to never trust influencers or celebrities. Time and time again I’ve seen World Class Boxers, douche bag YouTubers, and sound deaf Music Artists scam their own supporters without hesitation. It’s comical at this point when another project by Floyd “I need to steal money to have money” Mayweather comes out, or when you see Logan Paul squirm when Coffeezilla outed him. There’s been times where I’ve thought there’s no way this is real life, I guess that’s just Crypto/Web3 for you. 

As you can imagine, Bored Ape Yacht Club is full of celebrities endorsing the brand.  To speak on a few from the list we can start with DJ Khaled who from his Instagram posts would make you think he’s a godly man, but would a godly man accept money to promote a project that would turn out to be a scam? Well, he did with Bored Bunny NFT, then went on to delete the post after the project started getting attention in regards to being one (a scam). It’s ok though, Khaled, he sees everything, and the internet is forever as well. On the same note there’s French Montana who also promoted Bored Bunny. What’s funny about ol’ Frenchy though is the fact that he tried to sell his Ape 3 months after getting it. Then we have Logan Paul who’s done his own scam by the name of CryptoZoo that Coffeezilla did a 3 part video series on. The list goes on, like Jimmy Fallon who instead of showing empathy w

hen Julian Assange was dragged out of the embassy, tried making a joke of it. If only Jimmy had the ability or courage to report on real news. It’s ok though, most of them are getting sued anyway. Moral of the story, these people don’t care about you, you’re family, or what happens to you in your life. They’re selfish with no other interest than getting rich off their own community’s that support them. Why? Because they know the short-term memory people have in today’s day and age, they’re not scared of having to replace you with someone else because they know they can.

At the end of the day, could my assumption be wrong regarding it being a scam? Yes. Could my assumption be wrong about the founders being racist? Yes. What can’t be wrong is the facts that I’ve explained throughout the past two articles. The facts surrounding how this project has been ran, the celebrities that have endorsed it, and the ties to antisemitic traits that have appeared in the collection. What else is true? How about one of their biggest investors being FTX, the same company that stole over $8 billion dollars from its own customers. There are some interesting facts there as well that I look forward to telling you about.
