Linear A · Inscriptions and evidence

This section tracks structural analysis of the repository’s Linear A transcriptions. It is not a decipherment claim. Semantic mappings remain tentative unless independently supported.

Key takeaway The corpus analysis finds recurring patterns in sign order. Individual inscriptions also preserve checkable totals, recognizable goods, and clues to ceremonial language.

What can we read in Linear A?

A scribe’s arithmetic can confirm what a line means even when we cannot pronounce every word. A dedication leaves more room for interpretation. The confidence judgments below come from the inscriptions and their archaeological settings.

ZA 15 · A wine account from Zakros

KU-RO · WINE 78 — “Wine total: 78.” This reading is well supported. KU-RO marks sums across several tablets. WINE stands for a sign depicting a commodity. Its spoken Minoan name is unknown.

The preceding line reads KA-DI · WINE 3. A close rendering is “Kadi: three units of wine.” Kadi also appears beside 3 on ZA 4. It may identify a person, place, or account. Whether the wine was owed, received, or issued remains open. See the drawing of ZA 15b and compare ZA 4a.

Check the two wine totals

The first five entries give 57 + 10 + 3 + 6 + 2 = 78. The last four give 5 + 4 + 5 + 3 = 17. A stroke after the 2 falls exactly at the boundary. Reading it as a divider reproduces both totals on the reverse. Reading it as a half gives 78½ for the first group and still gives 17 for the second. Editors disagree about the stroke. The division into two groups holds up under either reading. Examine the front or read Barbara Montecchi’s discussion.

HT 31 · A possible word for a cup

PA-TA-QE appears beside a small, open vessel drawn without a handle and the quantity 3,000. The preceding entry pairs a similar outline with SU-PA₃-RA and 300. Our working gloss for PA-TA-QE is “a type of small handleless cup.” The count shows it was common in this inventory. Its Minoan name could refer to its shape, capacity, material, or use. A second inventory pairing the word with a recognizable vessel would test the reading. Compare the vessels on HT 31 and see Malcolm Wiener’s discussion of conical cups.

SY Za 2 · An uncertain reading from Syme

The four signs JA-SU-MA-TU occur after the opening formula. Editors disagree on the next mark. Reading it as the olive sign allows “Jasumatu presents olives” if the opening is a verb and Jasumatu is the giver. Reading it as RE produces the longer sequence JA-SU-MA-TU-RE and removes the evidence for olives in that position.

Another proposal connects SU-MA with a place and interprets the expression as referring to someone from Suma. The ancient place-name and the participant’s identity are uncertain. We leave the sequence untranslated. Compare the competing readings and the geographical proposal.
