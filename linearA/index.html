What is Linear A?

About 3,500 years ago, people on the Greek island of Crete kept records in a script we call Linear A. We can read many of their numbers. Most of their words are still a mystery.

How did the writer count the wine?

On a clay tablet from Zakros, the first five wine entries give these amounts. Try adding them. 57 + 10 + 3 + 6 + 2 = 78. On the other side, the writer put two signs, KU-RO, beside the wine sign and the number 78.

KU and RO are the labels researchers use for the two signs. The hyphen shows where one sign ends and the next begins. The same pair appears beside sums on other tablets. That gives us a good reason to read this line as “Wine total: 78.”

Check the marks on the wine tablet

The catalogue calls this tablet ZA 15. A small stroke after the 2 could mark the end of the list or mean one half. The first reading gives 78 exactly. The second gives 78½. The next four amounts add to 17 either way, matching the other total on the back.

One entry reads KA-DI beside wine and 3. “Kadi, three units of wine” is about as far as we can go. Kadi could be a person, a place or an account.
