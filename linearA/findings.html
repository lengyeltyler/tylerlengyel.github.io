Findings from the tablets

Arithmetic checks an accounting term. Repeated labels suggest how scribes organized their records. Each interpretation below includes a way to test it.

Seven entries and a total

On side B the seven amounts are 3, 3, 8, 2, 2, 2 and 4. They add to 24, exactly the figure following KU-RO. Six of the seven labels also occur on side A.

The shared labels suggest two accounts drawn from a common register. The amounts and order can change. A comparable pair with readable headings could reveal whether the distinction concerns a commodity, a transaction, or its timing. Inspect HT 9 and its sources.

The same six labels on both faces

On side B, A-DU heads six entries of 10. Side A assigns four of those labels 10 again, but SA-RU has 20 and QE-RA₂-U has 7. The sums of the preserved amounts are 60 and 67 respectively.

A fixed assessment compared with an actual allocation is one possible explanation. On HT 86a, DA-ME and MI-NU-TE follow A-DU with 20 each. That comparison rules out a general rule that A-DU requires ten. The direction of the transactions remains open. Inspect HT 95 and its sources.

Related spellings entered separately

On side A, TA-NA-TE has 2 and A-TA-NA-TE has 1. A-KU-MI-NA and A-DU-KU-MI-NA each have 1. Side B includes the wine sign and repeats DU-RE-ZA-SE from side A.

The separate entries make it risky to normalize away initial A or internal DU. The forms could identify distinct people or related words. To test whether A has a grammatical function, look for the same alternation across multiple stems in comparable contexts. Inspect ZA 10 and its sources.

A recurring pair of account labels

DA-ME immediately precedes MI-NU-TE on both faces of HT 95 and under A-DU on HT 86a. Three appearances represent only two tablets. If scribes followed a customary register, the order should recur when both labels appear in another comparable list. The pair could also reflect copying between records.

The two labels can occur separately. MI-NU-TE accompanies *303 and 6 on HT 106, while DA-ME accompanies A584 and 74 on HT 120. Neither word can safely be translated as “ten” or as a fixed ration. Damage limits what can be said about missing entries. HT 86a · HT 106 · HT 120.

Why the earlier score was retired

The May 2026 run counted 2,901 observations. Of these, 2,698 came from the external import and 203 from local write-ups. HTML and Markdown versions repeated some of the same examples. HT 31 and HT 95 even shared purported grain and oil lines despite documenting different records. The 71.36-point score therefore cannot establish how well the model describes the surviving inscriptions.

The external import also mixes words, sign groups and parts of lines. Identifiers can distinguish two faces of a single object. The 2,593 normalized entries and 619 identifiers in the archived run are counts in that dataset. A new evaluation needs to group rows by physical object before splitting training and test sets. The previous outputs remain available for inspection.
