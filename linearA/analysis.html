Can a computer spot the order of the signs?

We tested a program on Linear A records. Its best model gave the same result after we scrambled the signs within each entry. That makes it a poor guide to word order.

What went into the test?

The first test used 2,901 entries. Of these, 203 came from our own write-ups. Some examples appeared twice, and some notes assigned the same lines to two different tablets. We ran the test again using only the 2,698 entries from outside sources.

An entry can be a word, a group of signs or part of a line. Several entries can come from the same piece of clay.

What did the score tell us?

Removing the write-ups barely changed the program’s score. It went from 71.36 to 71.40 points. These points measure how the program did on our chosen tests. They cannot tell us the chance that a translation is right.

A better test would set whole tablets aside before training the program. That way, it could never learn from the front of a tablet and earn points for recognizing the back.

Numbers for repeating the test

The November 2026 run used seed 20260224. After standardizing spellings, it had 2,550 entries and 610 identifiers. The May run had 2,593 entries and 619 identifiers. An identifier can refer to one side of a tablet. The best model gave the original and shuffled entries identical likelihoods.
